What each metric means

wbgt — heat stress

Wet-bulb globe temperature folds air temperature, humidity, sun load, and wind into one heat-stress number — the standard used for athletics. It omits your fitness, acclimatization, and pace: the word beside the number is the reading, and the bands follow the National Weather Service — low, elevated, moderate, high heat stress, extreme caution, extreme (a veto at 90°F+).

source: computed from the NWS gridpoint forecast — a forecast value, not measured on the path.
dew point — humidity

The absolute moisture in the air, which sets how well sweat can evaporate. Below 55°F is comfortable; at 70°F+ evaporation stalls and heart rate drifts up at any pace. Tonight it holds at 73–74° — oppressive — and only eases to 70° by dawn. It omits temperature itself; that is what WBGT is for.

source: NWS gridpoint forecast (forecast).
rh — relative humidity

How full the air is compared with what it could hold at its current temperature. It swings with temperature even when moisture does not — tonight it climbs from 61% to 88% by dawn while the dew point barely moves. Read dew point for how the air feels; RH mostly explains why dawn grass is wet and laundry never dries.

source: NWS gridpoint forecast (forecast).
uv index

The EPA scale for how fast unprotected skin burns — at today’s peak of 9 (very high), damage begins in about 15 minutes. It says nothing about heat: the 5–8 AM window is UV 0 yet still humid. Shade, cover, and sunscreen change your exposure, not the index.

source: Open-Meteo model — an estimated value, not a measurement.
aqi — air quality

The EPA Air Quality Index, read from the worst nearby monitor and its dominant pollutant — today PM2.5 at 62, moderate. A protective reading, not an average. Moderate (51–100) is fine for most runners; unusually sensitive people may shorten hard sessions. 201+ strikes the hour from the ledger.

now: EPA AirNow monitor (measured) · hourly forecast: CAMS model (estimated).
wind & gusts

Sustained speed plus gusts, with the tick showing where the wind is headed. In heat, wind is a friend — it moves saturated air off your skin — but today’s S 6 (gusts 12) is too little to matter. It omits direction changes along a loop; see the route note for exposure.
