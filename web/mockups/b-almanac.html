What each column measures

WBGT — heat stress on a working body forecast · NWS

Wet-bulb globe temperature folds air temperature, humidity, sun load, and wind into one number for a body producing heat — the standard used by race medical directors and the military. It omits your fitness, acclimatization, and pace. Bands (NWS): under 80°F low, 80–85 moderate, 85–88 high, 88–90 extreme caution, 90+ we advise against running at all. Values come from the NWS forecast gridpoint for this park; when the NWS field is missing we compute an estimate and label it so.

Dew point — how well sweat works forecast · NWS

Dew point is absolute moisture in the air, and it governs whether sweat evaporates. Unlike relative humidity it does not swing with temperature through the day. Under 55°F is comfortable, 60–65 noticeable, 65–70 sticky, 70–75 oppressive, 75+ severely limits cooling. Today’s 73° means your effort will feel one to two gears harder than the pace suggests.

UV index forecast · Open-Meteo

The WHO/EPA ultraviolet scale. 0–2 low, 3–5 moderate, 6–7 high, 8–10 very high, 11+ extreme. It measures skin and eye exposure risk, not heat — a cool clear noon can still burn. Forecast hourly by Open-Meteo; sunscreen and a cap advised from 6 upward on exposed routes.

AQI — air quality monitored · AirNow

The EPA Air Quality Index. We show the worst reading among nearby official monitors, across all pollutants, with the dominant pollutant named — the protective framing. 0–50 good, 51–100 moderate, 101–150 unhealthy for sensitive groups, 151–200 unhealthy, 201+ we advise against outdoor exercise. Current values are monitored (AirNow); hourly forecasts are modeled (CAMS) and labeled as such.

Wind and gusts forecast · NWS

Sustained speed with direction, plus gusts when notable. Below 10 mph wind barely registers on a run; 10–20 costs seconds per mile into it; gusts above 30 make bridges and waterfront paths unpleasant. In heat, a little wind helps — it is the only cooling you get when the dew point is high.

Precipitation and thunder forecast · NWS

Chance of measurable precipitation for each hour. Rain is a comfort call; lightning is not — when the chance of thunder reaches 30% or storms appear in the forecast weather, we mark the hour not recommended and say why. There is no safe outdoor place in a thunderstorm.
