What each metric means

WBGT — heat stress

Wet-bulb globe temperature folds air temperature, humidity, sun load, and wind into one heat-stress number — the standard used for athletics. It omits your fitness, acclimatization, and pace. Words follow the National Weather Service bands: low, elevated, moderate, high heat stress, extreme caution, extreme (a veto at 90°F+).

source: NWS gridpoint forecast (forecast value, not estimated)

Dew point — humidity

The absolute moisture in the air, which sets how well sweat can evaporate. Below 55°F is comfortable; at 70°F+ evaporation stalls and heart rate drifts up at any pace. It omits temperature itself — that is what WBGT is for.

source: NWS gridpoint forecast

UV index

The EPA scale for sunburn speed on exposed skin — at UV 9, damage begins in about 15 minutes. It says nothing about heat: dawn hours can be brutal for WBGT yet UV 0. Shade, cover, and sunscreen change your exposure, not the index.

source: Open-Meteo model (forecast)

AQI — air quality

The EPA Air Quality Index, taken from the worst nearby monitor and its dominant pollutant — a protective reading, not an average. Moderate (51–100) is fine for most runners; unusually sensitive people may shorten hard sessions. 201+ vetoes the hour.

now: AirNow monitor (measured) · hourly forecast: CAMS model (modeled)

Wind & gusts

Sustained speed plus gusts. In heat, wind helps — it moves saturated air off your skin. Today’s S 6 is too little to matter. It omits direction changes along a loop; see the route note for exposure.

source: NWS gridpoint forecast

Rain & thunder

Chance of measurable precipitation for the hour. Rain alone rarely cancels a run; lightning always does — any hour with thunder probability at 30% or more is struck from the ledger with the reason named, never averaged away.

source: NWS gridpoint forecast (probabilityOfThunder, weather layer)
