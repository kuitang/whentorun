What these numbers mean
WBGT — wet bulb globe temperature NWS · forecast
The heat-stress standard used by race directors and the military: it folds temperature, humidity, sun load and wind into one thermometer reading. It does not know your pace or acclimatization. Values here come from the NWS gridpoint forecast (converted from °C); when the grid is missing, we compute an estimate and label it so. At 88–90°F organizers shorten or cancel workouts; above 90°F we mark hours not recommended.
Dew point — how the air feels NWS · forecast
The humidity number that matters for runners: sweat stops evaporating as dew point climbs. Under 60°F is comfortable, 65–70°F is very humid, above 70°F is oppressive — expect a slower pace at the same effort. Tonight stays near 73–74°F; tomorrow pre-dawn drops to 70°F, which is as good as this air mass gets.
UV index Open-Meteo · forecast
The WHO/EPA scale for sunburn risk. 0–2 needs no protection, 8+ ("Very High") burns exposed skin in under 15 minutes. UV is the one metric that is always zero before dawn — another argument for the 5 AM window. Forecast from Open-Meteo; the NWS grid does not carry UV.
AQI — air quality index AirNow · monitored
EPA index from the worst nearby AirNow monitor, taking the highest pollutant — today that is PM2.5 at 62, "Moderate": fine for most runners, noticeable if you are sensitive. Hourly forecast values are modeled (CAMS) and labeled as such. We mark hours at 201+ ("Very Unhealthy") not recommended.
Wind NWS · forecast
Sustained speed and direction, with gusts when notable. Light south air at 6 mph does little for cooling today; in winter the same table drives wind-chill vetoes below −10°F. Direction matters most on the exposed river paths.
Precipitation & thunder NWS · forecast
Chance of measurable rain each hour. Rain alone never vetoes a run; lightning does — any hour with thunderstorm probability at 30% or higher is marked not recommended by name, tonight 7–8 PM included. Warning-class alerts (severe thunderstorm, flash flood) also veto outright.
Central Park · route notes Good tree shade on the loop’s east and north sides; the reservoir path is exposed. Hills concentrate at the north end. Drains well after rain. Before dawn, the park drives are car-free and lit — run the main loop, not the bridle trails.

Clothing — Singlet, split shorts, cap; carry fluids for anything over 40 minutes at this dew point. For the 5 AM window: the same, plus something reflective for the dark.
